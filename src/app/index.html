A printable bone model from a CT scan

Drop in a CT study — shoulder, elbow, wrist, hand, or anything else — and get back a solid, watertight model at true size, ready to slice. The work is done by this page, on this computer. The scan is not uploaded.

Drop the scan folder here

The whole CD or export folder is fine; so are loose DICOM files or a .zip. The best series is chosen for you.

Reading files…

1 Identifiers are discarded

Names, hospital numbers, dates and every other identifying tag are dropped as each file is read. Only the image geometry and pixel values are kept — in memory, until you close the tab.

2 The bone is found for you

The page picks the thinnest series, removes the couch and whatever isn't the limb, sets the threshold from the scan itself and fills the marrow. The sliders are there if you disagree.

3 It prints in one piece

Loose bones are joined by short pegs and cut ends are capped. Download STL or 3MF, open it in Bambu Studio, add supports, print.

You don't have to take the privacy claim on trust. This page is served with a rule that forbids it from making any network connection, and your browser enforces it. Open the developer tools, watch the Network tab, and load a scan: after the page itself has loaded, nothing else is requested.

For teaching, surgical planning aids and talking with patients. Not a medical device and not for diagnosis — check the model against the original images.

Printing

STL opens in every slicer. 3MF is smaller and carries millimetre units — Bambu Studio will say it “is not from Bambu Lab”; choose to load the geometry.

Bambu A1 print tips
  • In Bambu Studio use Auto orient or Lay on face, then enable tree supports (auto).
  • 0.16 mm layers and 3 walls give crisp cortical detail; 10–15 % gyroid infill is plenty.
  • PLA is ideal. Add a brim if only a small area touches the plate.
  • Pegs (blue in the preview) hold loose bones together — snip them after printing if you want the bones separate.
